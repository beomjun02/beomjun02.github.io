  • 🆕 The Severance Problem: LLMs are Unaware of the Person Beyond the Prompt
    arXiv cs.CL · 2026-07-19 · Alignment · SafetyArgues that sycophancy, overconfidence, and hallucination stem from a fundamental limitation: LLMs lack explicit representation of what remains unknown about the user. Across five model families, the schema consistently reduces sycophancy and harmful advice, and models ask clarifying questions instead of confidently extrapolating.

  • 🆕 LLM Evaluators are Biased across Languages
    arXiv cs.CL · 2026-07-19 · Calibration · TrustworthyExperiments with semantically identical instruction-response pairs across 23 languages reveal that multilingual evaluators assign significantly different scores to different languages. Lower-resource languages are scored more generously — up to 43% difference in acceptance rate under a global threshold. Bias is invisible to pairwise accuracy (above 90%) and cannot be explained by content difficulty alone, meaning harmful content in lower-resource languages is more likely to pass safety filters.

  • 🆕 Implicit Reasoning Steering via Concept Chaining
    arXiv cs.CL · 2026-07-19 · Safety · AlignmentShows that natural-looking text can systematically steer LLM predictions without explicit instructions or direct answer cues. Concept Chaining generates short connection paragraphs linking question entities to a target answer through intermediate concepts, then continues pretraining the victim model on them. The steering is substantially less inferable than direct paraphrases, revealing a practical channel through which latent biases can be amplified by ordinary-looking text.
  • 🆕 Breaking Refusal in the First Half: A Mechanistic Study of the Prefill Jailbreak
    arXiv cs.CL · 2026-07-19 · SafetyLocalizes prefill jailbreak failure to an early response window: the first half of the response suffices to break refusal while the second half is nearly inert.   • 🆕 Semantic Register Compression in Multi-Agent LLM Cascades

  • 🆕 Reward-Free Evolving Agents via Pairwise Validator
    arXiv cs.AI · 2026-07-19 · Agent · Agent LearningReplaces per-step scalar reward in self-evolving agent loops with a frozen LLM pairwise validator that gives binary “which is better” verdicts. Integrated into three published self-evolving engines (GEPA, ADRS, ShinkaEvolve), the method matches or exceeds full-reward baselines without labeling cost.
