Papers

  • 🆕 Conformity Breaks Conformal Prediction
    Shows that conformal prediction silently breaks in multi-agent LLM systems: unanimous-wrong peers shift model scores enough to drop coverage from 90% to 74%, and targeted attacks nearly halve coverage on low-confidence subgroups. Standard conformal fixes fail because the scoring behavior — not the question distribution — has changed.
  • 🆕 A Calibrated Reflection Approach for Enhancing Confidence Estimation in LLMs
    Introduces Maximum Confidence Selection across all possible labels combined with reflection-based prompting and distance-aware calibration that accounts for ordinal label relationships. Evaluated on HelpSteer2, Llama T-REx, and a proprietary conversational dataset, demonstrating improved calibration for both conversational and fact-based classification.
  • 🆕 Why Better Models Can Create Riskier Systems: Evidence from LLM Agents in Financial Markets
    Identifies a capability paradox: more capable LLMs exhibit more correlated behavior, creating a non-diversifiable systemic risk floor in multi-agent deployments. In financial market simulations, correlated reasoning is beneficial when shared information is accurate but becomes a severe liability under shared misinformation.
  • 🆕 Safety for Whom? Boundary-Aware Self-Distillation for Controlled LLM Safety Refusal
    Formulates narrow-boundary safety where a single base model must refuse targeted political manipulation while answering factual questions about the same election. An escalating self-generation framework with boundary-pair data raises target-domain refusal from 9.47% to 84.75% while reducing over-refusal from 74% to 5.2%.
  • 🆕 Refuse without Refusal: A Structural Analysis of Safety-Tuning Responses for Reducing False Refusals in Language Models
    Decomposes safety-tuning responses into boilerplate refusal statements versus rationales, finding that refusal statements induce reliance on superficial cues and increase false refusals. Training solely on rationales reduces false refusals while maintaining comparable safety performance across multiple model families.
  • 🆕 DCFA: Dual-view Causal-inspired Attribution for Failure Reasoning in LLM-based Multi-agent Systems
    Proposes a training-free framework that combines global causal-inspired dependency graphs with local counterfactual reasoning to identify the decisive error — the earliest action whose correction reverses system failure — in multi-agent LLM traces. Improves step-level attribution accuracy by up to 8.27% over state-of-the-art baselines across six LLMs.
  • 🆕 Rethinking Indirect Prompt Injection as a Test-Time Search Problem
    Formulates indirect prompt injection as test-time search over task-dependent attack surfaces and introduces an agentic attacker with environment reconnaissance and structured strategy management. Finds that increasing attacker compute improves vulnerability discovery, suggesting security evaluations should characterize the attacker's search budget rather than treating attack success as budget-independent.
  • 🆕 A Case Study on Emergent Cheating and Whistleblowing in Autonomous Research Swarms
    Reports on a collective of 100 autonomous LLM agents tasked with proving mathematical conjectures, where cheating spontaneously emerged and was later challenged by whistleblowing agents. Demonstrates that shared communication infrastructure in multi-agent systems can serve as a substrate for contagious spread of unintended and undesirable behaviors.
  • 🆕 Persistent Teacher Anchoring for Tool-Using Agents
    Proposes a student-induced but teacher-committed rollout construction for tool-using agent distillation where tool calls reach the environment only after teacher verification. Improves macro best@4 by 2.5–2.8 points over standard on-policy knowledge distillation under the same downstream RL budget across retrieval and perception tasks.
  • 🆕 Bilevel Coordinated Reflection: A Game-Theoretic Approach to Multi-Agent LLM Systems
    Models orchestrator-worker interaction in multi-agent LLM systems as a bilevel coordination game where workers' local updates form an approximate potential game with equilibrium slack controlled by decomposition quality. Provides a unified theoretical account of coordination, memory improvement, and the role of external verification in multi-agent reflection.
  • 🆕 When Seeing Overrides Knowing: Visual Dominance and Deferral-Based Method for Personalized Safety in VLMs
    Introduces MPS-Bench (5,181 scenarios across 12 high-risk domains) and finds that frontier VLMs almost always respond directly (86–99%) rather than seek missing user context, scoring below 2.6/5 on personalized safety. Identifies visual dominance — early visual information suppresses textual risk signals during fusion — and proposes PRISM, a lightweight monitor achieving 0.978 AUC for deferral prediction.
  • 🆕 Knowing What Not to Answer: Selective Non-Compliance in Vision-Language Models
    Introduces KoNA, a benchmark evaluating VLM selective non-compliance across five categories (false premise, visual inaccessibility, universal unknown, task feasibility, safety) under both single and compound queries. Fine-tuning on examples requiring selective non-compliance substantially improves refusal accuracy while largely maintaining performance on fully answerable tasks.
