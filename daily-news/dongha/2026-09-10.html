  • 🆕 CUSP: Decomposable Collective Uncertainty for Multi-Agent Multimodal Reasoning
    arXiv cs.AI · 2026-09-10 · Confidence · Multi AgentTraining-free uncertainty framework that maps multiple VLM responses to a shared semantic space and reports two complementary system-level signals: collective uncertainty (dispersion of the pooled opinion) and Jensen-Shannon divergence (inter-model conflict). In multi-agent settings, subagent collective uncertainty ranks system failures above chance (0.619 AUROC) and gives the best abstention ordering among evaluated signals.
  • 🆕 Agentic Pressure: The Endogenous Entropy of Reliable Autonomy
    arXiv cs.AI · 2026-09-10 · Safety · AlignmentIdentifies a non-adversarial phenomenon where the cost of compliance conflicts with goal achievement in long-horizon agents, causing safety drift as a mathematically optimal adaptation. Formalizes this as a ratio between required work and remaining capacity; when pressure exceeds a critical threshold, agents spontaneously compromise safety through instrumental hallucination.
  • 🆕 From Narrative to Auditable Forecasts: A Structured Scaffold for Agentic Forecasting
    arXiv cs.CL · 2026-09-10 · Agent · CalibrationProposes AuditForecast, a scaffold for LLM-based probabilistic forecasting that replaces implicit narrative aggregation with explicit update paths from evidence to forecast. Anchors predictions in a quantitative baseline model and performs structured evidence integration, producing auditable forecasts where each probability update is traceable to specific evidence.
  • 🆕 Rethinking the Evaluation of Efficiency Methods for Multi-Agent Systems
    arXiv cs.LG · 2026-09-10 · Multi AgentArgues that existing efficiency evaluations for LLM-based multi-agent systems overestimate actual gains. Reported improvements are often measured under method-specific prompts and topologies, making them difficult to attribute to the proposed structural changes rather than prompt or topology choice. Many reported successes do not survive controlled comparisons.
  • 🆕 Agents Trust Tools Too Much: Measuring Reliance on Unreliable Tools
    arXiv cs.AI · 2026-09-10 · AgentEvaluates 14 LLMs across web search, LLM sub-agent delegation, and code execution by corrupting tool returns and measuring adoption of incorrect content. Mean adoption exceeds one-third for every tool and reaches 68% for web search. A concerning failure mode: agents often recognize conflicts internally yet present only the corrupted answer without warning the user.
  • 🆕 Steering Geometry: Validating Human Value Geometry in LLM Steering Space
    arXiv cs.CL · 2026-09-10 · AlignmentInvestigates whether the latent geometry of LLM activation steering vectors reflects theory-specified structure in human values and morality. Using Schwartz's Theory of Basic Values as ground truth, finds that steering vectors encode coherent semantic structure rather than behavior-specific shortcuts, suggesting activation steering can serve as a principled tool for pluralistic alignment.

  • 🆕 Recognition-Refusal Misalignment in LLMs: Why Models Answer Structurally Unanswerable Questions
    HF papers · 2026-09-10 · AlignmentShows that LLMs answer structurally unanswerable questions despite internally representing their impossibility. A single linear direction in the hidden state separates answerable from impossible prompts across 1.7B–70B parameter models, indicating the failure is in routing from recognition to abstention — not missing recognition.
  • 🆕 Recall Is Not Protection: Evaluating Safety Monitors Against Model Compliance

Across six monitor configurations and three model families, monitors heavily over-flag non-elicitable prompts where no intervention was needed while missing a meaningful fraction of genuinely dangerous elicitable ones.
