  • 🆕 τ₀-VLA: Hierarchical Robot Foundation Model with World-Model-Guided Test-Time Computation
    A hierarchical VLA architecture that integrates a world model to guide test-time computation, enabling the robot to reason about future states before acting. Combines the strengths of VLA generalization with world-model planning for more robust multi-task manipulation.
  • 🆕 HiTac-WAM: Hierarchical Tactile World Action Model for Contact-Rich Manipulation
    A world action model that forecasts hierarchical tactile states — contact state, 3D deformation fields, and slip — for each candidate action before execution. Achieves 72.2% success rate on real robots for contact-rich manipulation tasks that require fine-grained touch reasoning.
  • 🆕 Fine-Tuning VLAs with Self-Demonstrated Generative Control
    Addresses the critical problem of VLA finetuning on new embodiments: naïve finetuning on expert data improves the target task but destroys instruction-following and behavioral priors. This method uses self-demonstrated zero-shot rollouts to generate training data, preserving the VLA's generalist capabilities while adapting to new hardware.
  • 🆕 OrthoSkillVLA: Continual Skill Learning via Gradient-Informed Skill Subspace Adaptation
    Tackles catastrophic forgetting when sequentially adapting pretrained VLAs to diverse skills. Uses gradient-informed orthogonal subspaces that respect each skill's unique geometry, avoiding the growing inference footprint of architecture-based isolation while maintaining previously learned skills.
  • 🆕 EXIMO: VLM-Guided Exploration for Efficient VLA Finetuning
    Proposes an explore-imitate-optimize loop where a VLM guides a VLA policy's exploration, generating useful rollouts without expensive human teleoperation. Dramatically reduces the human labor needed to finetune VLA policies for new tasks, combining VLM reasoning with RL-based policy improvement.
  • 🆕 QDOS: Quality-Diversity Offline Skill Learning
    Combines quality-diversity optimization with offline RL to extract diverse, high-performing skill policies from pre-collected datasets. The two-stage approach first learns diverse low-level skills via QD methods rather than unsupervised trajectory VAEs, then trains a high-level policy — showing improved coverage and performance on manipulation and locomotion tasks.
