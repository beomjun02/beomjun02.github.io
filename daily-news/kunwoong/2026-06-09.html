Papers

  • SS-TPT: Stability and Suitability-Guided Test-Time Prompt Tuning for Adversarially Robust Vision-Language Models (2606.06943
    Evaluates the quality of augmented views via stability (prediction invariance to weak augmentations) and suitability (feature-space density) scores. Both scores guide adaptation and inference through consistency loss and weighted prediction, amplifying trustworthy views while suppressing corrupted ones. Outperforms prior state-of-the-art on robustness-throughput trade-offs across diverse datasets and view counts.
  • MotionEnhancer: Leveraging Video Diffusion for Motion-Enhanced Vision-Language Models (2606.06853
    Distills motion priors from a video diffusion model to enhance VLM motion understanding via attention alignment. Two parameter-free modules — Motion-sensitive Head Selection and Motion-salient Text Token Identification — extract and optimize motion-related attention in a computation-only manner, no extra parameters. Consistent improvements on motion-level video understanding benchmarks.
  • Diagnosing Visual Ignorance in Vision-Language Models (2606.06890
    Combines counterfactual layer replacement with supervised MLP probing to trace how visual vs. language-prior signals compete across the decoder. Reveals a multi-stage bottleneck: intermediate layers fail to retrieve visual info, while later layers suppress surviving visual signals. A progressive visual decay metric shows a substantial fraction of VQA benchmark examples remain answerable under total visual obfuscation — benchmarks can inadvertently reward visual ignorance.
  • ThinkBooster: A Unified Framework for Seamless Test-Time Scaling of LLM Reasoning (2606.06915
    A modular Python library implementing state-of-the-art test-time compute scaling strategies (multi-sample generation, verifier-based reranking) with a benchmark that jointly evaluates performance and computational efficiency. Includes an OpenAI-compatible proxy service for drop-in adaptive reasoning in production. Empirical results on math and code reveal performance-compute trade-offs across TTC methods.
  • DyCon: Dynamic Reasoning Control via Evolving Difficulty Modeling (2606.07108
    Shows that problem difficulty evolves dynamically during reasoning and is linearly encoded in the LRM's step-level embeddings. Proposes a training-free framework that leverages these latent representations to dynamically control reasoning depth, mitigating overthinking. Reduces redundant steps without sacrificing accuracy across 4 models (4B–32B) and 12 benchmarks in math, QA, and coding.
  • The Piggyback Hypothesis of Generalization: Explaining and Mitigating Emergent Misalignment (2606.06667
    Proposes that chat-template tokens piggyback finetuned behavior onto out-of-domain queries, causing emergent misalignment. Subtle perturbations to the prefix or patching prefix representations from the unfinetuned model restore alignment without changing the query. Token-Regularized Finetuning (TReFT) reduces emergent misalignment by 33.5% more than data interleaving on Llama-3.1-8B while preserving in-domain learning.
  • TRUST: Exploring Agentic Tool-Calling Decisions via Uncertainty-Aligned Reinforcement Learning (2606.06976
    Observes that decision-oriented RL weakens uncertainty separation between correct and incorrect actions, causing overconfident mistakes. Incorporates uncertainty quantification into reward design as a repulsive force maintaining separation, with lightweight key-turn annotations for unified multi-turn trajectory post-training. Consistently enhances decision quality while maintaining reliable uncertainty estimates during optimization.
  • OpenSkill: Open-World Self-Evolution for LLM Agents (2606.06741
    Bootstraps both skills and verification signals from scratch using open-world resources — documentation, repositories, the web — with no target-task supervision. Synthesizes transferable skills and refines them against self-built virtual tasks. Skills transfer across models without adaptation, and the self-built verifier aligns with ground-truth despite never accessing target answers.
  • Skip a Layer or Loop It? Learning Program-of-Layers in LLMs (2606.06574
    Reveals that pretrained layers can be packed as modules, skipped, or looped to form customized per-input execution programs. A lightweight prediction network learns to generate these programs dynamically. For most inputs, shorter programs match or improve accuracy; incorrect predictions can be corrected by alternative programs with fewer layers — fixed-depth execution captures only a narrow subset of an LLM's latent capacity.
  • Data-Constrained Language Model Pretraining: Improved Regularization and Scaling Laws (2606.06881
    Studies multi-epoch pretraining where data is limited. Masked-input regularization (MIR) — an auxiliary next-token loss on randomly masked inputs — improves validation loss over strong weight decay alone. Proposes SoftQ, a scaling law coupling model and data size to capture their interaction under repeated data. Estimates MIR's gains as equivalent to ~1.3x as much unique data.
  • SCALE: Scalable Cross-Attention Learning with Extrapolation for Agentic Workflow Scheduling (2606.06804
    A DRL scheduler for agentic LLM workflow DAGs that generalizes to unseen cluster scales without fine-tuning. Cross-attention pointer network lets the architecture accept any number of servers by construction. Structured Representation Regularization keeps feature statistics stable across scales — trained on 16 nodes, tested on 32 and 48, achieving 8.9% lower response time vs. the same architecture without regularization.
  • Data-Efficient Autoregressive-to-Diffusion Language Models via On-Policy Distillation (2606.06712
    Converts pretrained autoregressive LMs into diffusion language models via on-policy self-distillation: the student generates its own trajectories while the frozen original teacher provides target logits. Eliminates the train-inference mismatch of standard DLMs while enhancing knowledge retention. Requires 15x to 7,000x fewer training tokens with strong performance across tasks, positioning DLM conversion as a form of post-training.
