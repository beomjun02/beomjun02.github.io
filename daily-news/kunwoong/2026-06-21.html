  • 🆕 HydraHead: From Head-Level Functional Heterogeneity to Specialized Attention Hybridization
    arXiv cs.CL · 2026-06-21 · architectureHybridizes Full Attention and Linear Attention along the head axis rather than the conventional layer-wise approach, using interpretability-driven selection to identify retrieval-critical heads. A scale-normalized fusion module reconciles the distributional gap between FA and LA. At a 7:1 LA-to-FA ratio, matches the long-context performance of a 3:1 layer-wise hybrid, and approaches Qwen3.5’s 256K native context with only 15B training tokens.
  • 🆕 GEMS: Geometric Constraints Enable Multi-Semantic Superposition in LLMs
    arXiv cs.CL · 2026-06-21 · fundamentalIdentifies two independent sources of collapse when superposing multiple steering directions: distributional deviation and directional interference. Proposes training-free geometric constraints (norm-preserving superposition + real-time orthogonalization) that preserve 98% GSM8K accuracy when injecting three concurrent non-mathematical directions, vs. 4% with unconstrained addition. Transfers across architectures from 3B to 31B.

  • 🆕 Uncertainty Decomposition for Clarification Seeking in LLM Agents

  • 🆕 Mix-QVLA: Task-Evidence-Aware Mixed-Precision Quantization of Vision-Language-Action Models

  • 🆕 ViCoStream: Streaming VideoLLMs Can Run Beyond 100 FPS with Stage-Wise Coordinated Inference

  • ITNet: A Learnable Integral Transform That Subsumes Convolution, Attention, and Recurrence
    arXiv cs.AI · 2026-06-20 · architecture · fundamentalShows that convolution, self-attention, and autoregressive recurrence (LSTM, GRU, S4, Mamba) are all special cases of a single learnable integral transform. A shared ITNet operator with lightweight modality-specific encoders matches or exceeds specialized baselines on ImageNet-1K, GLUE, ModelNet40, and VQA v2 — demonstrating one interaction mechanism can recover all three architectural families from data.
  • GRACE: Granularity-Regulated Adaptive Computational Efficiency for Optimal Verification in Test-Time Scaling
    arXiv cs.AI · 2026-06-20 · test-timeEstablishes a unified framework characterizing optimal verification granularity as a function of problem difficulty, verifier accuracy, and compute budget. Proves a phase transition: fine-grained verification (PRMs) dominates when budget is large or problems are hard, while coarse-grained (ORMs) wins in low-budget easy regimes. Adaptive strategy outperforms fixed-granularity baselines by up to 3.1% accuracy at matched compute on MATH-500 and AIME.
  • Trustworthy Multi-Agent Systems: Mitigating Semantic Drift with the Argent Signaling Protocol
