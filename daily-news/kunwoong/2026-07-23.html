Papers

  • 🆕 A Controlled Study of Attention-Only Transformers
    arXiv cs.LG · 2026-07-23 · architecturePretrains attention-only decoder transformers (Simple Attention Networks, SANs) against standard transformers matched for params, FLOPs, and depth up to 48 layers. Deleting FFNs in place costs 0.47 nats, but reallocating the freed budget into attention depth closes the gap to 0.006 nats (0.27%) — reproducible across seeds and shrinking with scale. A clean necessity test for the FFN component.
  • 🆕 Dual Attention Residuals
    arXiv cs.CL · 2026-07-23 · architectureExtends transformer residual pathways via reciprocal cross-stream addressing: for each target stream, DAR computes depth weights from the opposite stream's normalized states and applies them to the target's own history. Bridges historical retrieval and multi-stream methods into a single mechanism — both capabilities were previously studied in isolation.
  • 🆕 Convolution for Large Language Models
    arXiv cs.CL · 2026-07-23 · architectureStudies whether lightweight depthwise convolutions can supply local inductive bias in Transformers. Ablates convolution at 17 locations in a Qwen3 block (Alibaba); best results when applied to projected Q, K, V before attention with kernel size k=3, residual, no extra norm. Improves accuracy across seven benchmarks while adding minimal parameters.
  • 🆕 Where Should Optimizer State Live? Tiered State Allocation for Memory-Efficient MoE Training
    arXiv · 2026-07-23 · MoEOn a 6.78B MoE model, AdamW keeps 50.6 GB of moments for 12.6 GB of weights. Proposes SkewAdam, which gives different optimizer state to the dense backbone, experts, and router based on their distinct gradient statistics. Directly targets the largest memory line item in MoE training.
  • Test-Time Scaling for World Action Models via Zero-Shot Geometric Evaluation
    arXiv · 2026-07-22 · test-timeProposes a training-free selective test-time scaling framework for WAMs that ranks sampled rollouts using geometric consistency of predicted future observations. Enables WAMs to decide when extra compute is worthwhile before executing an action, without additional training.
  • Understanding Reasoning from Pretraining to Post-Training
