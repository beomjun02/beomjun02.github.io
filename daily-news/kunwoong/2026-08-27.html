Papers

  • 🆕 PuzzleKV: Page-Wise Low-Rank Decomposition for KV Cache Compression
    Partitions each per-head KV cache into fixed-length logical pages and exploits substantial low-rank structure within individual pages. PuzzleKV decomposes completed pages via SVD independently per layer and head, computes attention directly over a mix of dense and factorized pages, and compresses incrementally during autoregressive decoding — no training or calibration data needed. At ~60% of original KV storage, it retains &gt;96% of full-KV performance across RULER and LongBench; combined with quantization, it reaches 93% at just 18.7% storage.
  • 🆕 VisCache: Visual KV Cache Pruning for Efficient Vision Large Language Model Inference
    A plug-and-play framework for coarse-to-fine visual KV cache pruning in VLLMs. A lightweight VLM first filters temporal redundancy by forwarding only semantically informative keyframes; then PruneKV applies parabolic layer-wise budget allocation with an asymmetric mechanism that selectively prunes keys while fusing values, preserving critical context. Achieves up to 2.35x speedup and significant memory reduction with only 19-28% KV cache retention while maintaining competitive performance.
  • 🆕 Minima-KV: Retention-Preserving KV Cache Compression with Mixed-Format Paged Attention
    A retention-preserving hierarchy for mixed-format paged attention: recent and anchor pages stay in FP8 while older pages move to packed TQ3, with format-specific kernels computing partial attention states merged through globally normalized online-softmax. On Qwen3.6-27B (single 96GB Blackwell GPU), achieves 3.50x compression relative to BF16 and 1.75x vs FP8 with matched quality on 16K RULER needle tasks and only 0.4-0.8pp delta on LongBench v2.
  • 🆕 MoTE: Mixture of Task Experts for Multi-Task Video Understanding
    Standard MoE decoders use token-level learned routing that isn't aligned with task-level procedural objectives in video understanding. MoTE introduces task-conditioned expert routing that directly aligns expert selection with heterogeneous procedural video tasks — action recognition, forecasting, and procedure prediction — providing conditional computation that respects task boundaries rather than treating all tokens uniformly.
  • 🆕 Giga-Embeddings: MoE Encoders for High-Throughput Text Embeddings
    A family of MoE text embedding models. The largest: a sparse 10B-parameter encoder with ~1.8B active parameters per token, achieving the strongest retrieval quality across English, Russian, multilingual, and code MTEB benchmarks while processing 114.5k tokens/sec (25% higher throughput than the dense 3B variant, 1.56-2.65x the throughput of evaluated external systems). A distilled 480M model surpasses FRIDA on Russian MTEB with 42% fewer parameters.
  • 🆕 HAP: Head-Adaptive Visual Token Pruning via Cross-Modal Alignment
    Existing VLM token pruning averages text-to-visual attention uniformly across all heads, but misaligned heads dominate the average and drown out fine-grained cues. HAP introduces PAQ (Prompt-Grounded Attention Quality) to quantify per-head alignment quality, then weights attention maps via PAQ-weighted softmax. On LLaVA-1.5-7B, retaining only 5.6% of visual tokens preserves 99.1% of original performance, beating AutoPrune by 4.2 points.
  • 🆕 StepGuard: Learning Step-Level Guardrails for LLM Agents
    A step-level guard model that can both audit completed agent trajectories and check tool actions before execution — addressing the gap where existing guardrails only evaluate finished trajectories, leaving pre-execution monitoring of individual tool invocations underexplored. Introduces scalable supervision methods for training guardrails that intercept risky actions in real time without requiring full-trajectory evaluation.
  • 🆕 Autonomous Mathematical Discovery in an Open-World Multi-Agent Environment
    AI agents from different model families pursue shared mathematical research in "the Station" — an open-world environment with no central coordinator or scripted pipeline. Agents choose their own research directions, conduct experiments, and build shared literature. Across 12 problems, they produced novel results on five: new infinite families of finite-field Kakeya sets, new 604-point kissing configurations in dimension 11, new records for discretized Kakeya needle, and improved bounds for Erdos's minimum-overlap problem.
