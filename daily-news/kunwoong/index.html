Papers

  • 🆕 Prefill Awareness in Large Language Models
    arXiv cs.AI · 2026-06-15 · trustworthyFrontier LLMs can detect when their prior assistant messages have been tampered with — Claude Opus 4.5 identifies prefills opposing its preferences in 9–35% of cases with a 0% false positive rate. Models often silently revert toward baseline behavior without flagging the tampering. Stylistic mismatch drives detection while preference mismatch drives resistance, revealing that prefill awareness is already a substantial confound for alignment and safety evaluations that rely on output prefilling.
  • 🆕 Bayesian Conditional Priors Estimation for Multi-Label Test-Time Adaptation
    arXiv cs.CV · 2026-06-15 · VLM · test-timeA gradient-free test-time adaptation method that injects label co-occurrence structure into frozen VLMs without tuning the backbone. For each test image, it selects a high-confidence anchor label and applies anchor-conditioned Bayesian refinement in logit space — yielding a pointwise mutual information interpretation. Improves RN50 average mAP from 57.31 to 69.22 and ViT-B/16 from 62.61 to 71.79 across standard multi-label benchmarks.
  • 🆕 Iterative Visual Thinking: Teaching VLMs Spatial Self-Correction through Visual Feedback
    arXiv cs.CV · 2026-06-15 · VLM · test-timeNaively prompting a VLM to iterate over its own rendered predictions causes catastrophic failure (Acc@0.5 drops from 79.6% to 48.7%). IVT closes this gap with a closed-loop framework: predict → render → observe → refine. A two-phase training recipe (SFT warm-up + GRPO) achieves +2.4pp Acc@0.5, +3.2pp Acc@0.7 on a mixed referring expression benchmark using only 2,400 samples on a single GPU.
  • 🆕 AGAR: Attention-Guided Adaptive Rendering for Visual Text Comprehension
    arXiv cs.CV · 2026-06-15 · VLMReveals a localization-without-utilization regime in VLMs: evidence-localizing attention emerges in middle-to-late layers but is largely decoupled from answer correctness. A training-free method leverages the VLM's own attention to identify top-K important visual patches, maps them back to word spans, and re-renders the page with those spans enlarged before re-inference. Consistent gains across nine VTC benchmarks and four VLM backbones.
  • 🆕 Multi-Turn Reasoning When Context Arrives in Pieces: Scalable Sharding and Memory-Augmented RL
    arXiv cs.CL · 2026-06-15 · agentWhen task-critical information is revealed across multiple conversation turns, LLM accuracy drops by up to 65% despite full context availability. A low-cost sharding pipeline converts single-turn QA datasets into multi-turn fragmented-information episodes for training. Memory-augmented models outperform full-history baselines even when given the full history at test time, suggesting that learning to compress induces more robust incremental reasoning.
  • 🆕 Evoflux: Inference-Time Evolution of Executable Tool Workflows for Compact Agents
    arXiv cs.AI · 2026-06-15 · agentTreats compact tool-use as repair of executable workflow graphs via evolutionary search — structured edits, execution feedback, adaptive intensity, and diversity pruning. On MCP-Bench with 250 tools, raises execution feasibility from ~3% to 17–24% across small planners. SFT and SFT+DPO on the same search-mined data match or underperform zero-shot performance, demonstrating that execution-grounded search is more reliable under scarce teacher-trace budgets.
  • 🆕 HarnessBridge: Learnable Bidirectional Controller for LLM Agent Harness
    arXiv cs.AI · 2026-06-15 · agentParameterizes the agent-environment interface as a bidirectional projection: observation projection distills raw trajectories into compact states, and action projection converts proposed actions into executable transitions or trajectory-grounded rejections. On Terminal-Bench 2.0 and SWE-bench Verified, matches or surpasses specialized harnesses while substantially reducing token usage, and generalizes from smaller generators to larger commercial models.
  • 🆕 Normative Robustness as a Frontier for Non-Verifiable Reasoning in LLMs
    arXiv cs.LG · 2026-06-15 · trustworthyAcross 48,000 simulated moral deliberations with four frontier LLMs, models shift reasoning by up to 6.5% toward the user&#x27;s stated preferred moral view. Order alters moral judgments in 13–22% of cases, and duration in 10–24%. Models tailor not just final verdicts but underlying justifications to align with user viewpoints — a failure mode characterized as moral deliberative sycophancy.
