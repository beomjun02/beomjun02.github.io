Experiment log · Dexterous Point Policy

Contact prediction generalizes past BMW; the 6-point abstraction survives DOF but not hand scale

Two threads. (1) The learned tactile-contact model, already shown on BMW, measured on PnP and Tool. (2) The two new hand swaps (Wuji 20-DOF, Sharpa 22-DOF) exercised in the OpenArm Isaac sim, then DPP rolled out in the PnP benchmark with each hand and scored by the harness's own success_at_end. Two claims from the first attempt (a success rate and a closed-loop clip) are retracted below, and the debugging turned up a real data problem: object clouds that smear.

Verdict

The tactile model validated on BMW transfers to PnP (micro-F1 0.70 vs manual GT) and to Tool, and on the spray power-grip it recovers the ring and pinky it under-fires on PnP pinches — it is not pinky-blind, the grasp just has to load the finger. On the sim side, DPP rolled out in the actual PnP benchmark scores 29.2 % with Inspire (reproducing the ≈27 % baseline bit-for-bit) and 0 % with both Wuji (20-DOF) and Sharpa (22-DOF) — and the cause is finger reach, not DOF count: the retargeting IK displaces the wrist by 2.8–5.4 cm to absorb the mismatch. Rescaling the fingertip targets fixes the IK (wrist residual → 0.4 cm, 94–96 % convergence) and still scores 0/24, because DPP's input is six points too. The 6-point abstraction is hand-agnostic in form but not in scale, on both sides. Two claims from the first attempt are withdrawn below (a pinned-object "3/3 lift", and a closed-loop clip where the arm never reached the bottle); chasing the second produced the most useful number of the day: in the fine-tune data the bottle's object cloud smears past 30 cm on 14.2 % of frames (median 8.3 cm) — a missing depth-gradient filter, and now the prime suspect for deployment failures.

Follow-up added 2026-07-31

The cross-hand result on this page was measured at 24 episodes per hand on a benchmark that turned out to have only four distinct initial conditions. It has been re-run on the corrected protocol (1 cm per-episode jitter) at 96 episodes per hand, and it reproduces: Inspire 25.0 % [17.4–34.5] against Wuji and Sharpa at 0/96 [0–3.85], now with non-overlapping intervals, and IK residuals matching these to within a millimetre under completely different starting poses.

That matters for how strongly the claim can be stated: at n=24 a zero only supported "at most 13.8 %"; at n=96 it supports "at most 3.85 %". See the 2026-07-31 log.

Why the two new hands score zero

Not the policy going idle, and not the DOF count. The retargeting IK cannot deliver what the policy asks, so the loop deadlocks before the hand ever touches the bottle.

A CPU-only retargeting study (1024 Inspire-FK target sets) predicted this before any rollout: mean wrist residual 0.19 cm (Inspire) / 1.51 (Sharpa) / 4.42 (Wuji), with all six sites inside the ~8 mm success band for 100 % / 74 % / 1.7 % of targets. The equal-weight IK pays for the finger-length mismatch by displacing the wrist. Palm orientation and curl direction were checked at 0 / 50 / 90 % flexion and are correct on all three.

The finding that matters for the 6-point abstraction

Shrinking the commanded fingertip targets about the wrist by each hand's finger-reach ratio collapses the IK error to Inspire-class — Wuji wrist residual 5.43 → 0.39 cm with 94 % convergence, Sharpa 2.77 → 0.48 cm with 96 % — and the arm then moves decisively. It still scores 0/24, in the wrong direction: DPP's input is also six points, so a scale-normalised small hand is out of distribution on the observation side too. The 6-point interface is hand-agnostic in form but not in scale, on both the action and the observation side. That is the concrete answer to decision D1 — Option A ("keep the abstraction") needs a scale-canonicalisation step on both sides, not just a retargeting fix.

Two integration bugs had to be fixed to get here, both worth remembering for the next hand: the MJCF-converted hand USDs nest links as …/Robot/openarm_base/<link> where Inspire has …/Robot/<link>, which breaks the contact sensor's last-/ path split; and activate.sh cannot auto-detect Isaac Lab on these nodes, so ISAACLAB must be exported.

Two claims from the first attempt, withdrawn

Retraction

An earlier version of this log reported "3/3 lift" per hand with a mean Δz. That number is withdrawn. The scene called write_root_state_to_sim() every step to hold the bottle at a fixed offset from the hand through the close and lift, so the object rose because the script moved it — the metric measured its own teleport, not a grasp. It also was not the task that matters (the PnP benchmark). No success rate should be read off that scene; the table at the top of this section replaces it.

The closed-loop attempt was degenerate — and why

A first closed-loop run of the trained policy (Inspire, ckpt 410000.pt) was also published here as working. It wasn't: the arm never approaches the bottle. Retracted, but kept because the diagnosis is the useful part.

  • →The policy was commanding "stay". Per-step target vs current wrist was 0.2–1.3 cm for all 200 steps, so nothing was asked of the arm.
  • →It was fed a frozen training point cloud. --override-obj-pts train_obj_pts_cellA_t0_from_h5.npy is a (4,128,3) cloud captured at t0 of a training episode — never updated during the rollout and unrelated to the live sim scene. In that particular frame the container slot is smeared to a 47 cm z-extent; two slots are zero padding.
  • ·The loop also ran 34× too slow. rate_violations=200/200, 1.68 s per step against an intended ~20 Hz. Contributing, but secondary to having no sensible object observation.
  • ✓IK was fine. Wrist 0.3–0.4 cm / finger 0.7–0.9 cm residuals sit inside our normal operating band (~8 mm); the ik_failures=0 counter only counts exceptions, so it is not a health check.

05Side finding — the object clouds smear

Chasing the frozen cloud above led somewhere more useful: the fine-tune data's own object points blow up on a sizeable minority of frames. Measured on 0501_pnp_500_v2_b0_shrink20/rlwrld_final.h5, 20 bottle episodes / 359 sampled frames, as the z-extent of each 128-point object cloud.

object slotmedian z-extentp90p99maxframes > 30 cm
slot 0 — bottle0.083 m0.357 m0.491 m0.596 m14.2 %
slot 1 — container0.183 m0.218 m0.358 m0.468 m2.5 %

A bottle is ~25 cm tall and this bin ~18 cm, so the medians are right — but the bottle cloud exceeds 30 cm on 14.2 % of frames (3–6× the object, up to 60 cm). That is the flying-pixel / depth-bleed signature: this h5 family has mask-side cleanup (b0, shrink20) but no depth-gradient filter. The --grad_thresh 0.010 filter built for the pretraining rebuild is the obvious fix to port onto the fine-tune extraction — and point quality, not IK and not the hand, is now the prime suspect for deployment failures.

06Reads & caveats

  • ✓Contact prediction is not BMW-specific. PnP micro-F1 0.70 vs manual GT; contact lands on the correct fingers at closure across five object types.
  • ✓The model isn't pinky-blind. Pinky F1 0.15 on PnP pinches but 0.51 on the spray power grip — it fires the pinky when the grasp loads it.
  • ✓Both new hands load and actuate in Isaac. Single articulation, all 20 / 22 finger joints track targets — the hand-swap plumbing works.
  • ✗The "3/3 lift" success rate is retracted. The object was pinned to the hand each step; the metric measured a scripted carry. Redone properly as DPP × PnP (§06).
  • →Tool manual GT is the weak link, not the model. It marks 11.5 % of frames and never labels the thumb — the microwave clip shows a correct door-handle grasp scored as disagreement. Use the auto/proximity reference for Tool.
  • →PnP pinky recall is low (0.08). Under-fires the pinky in pinch grasps; precision stays high (0.80), so it is a miss-rate, not a false-alarm, issue.
  • ✗The closed-loop DPP clip is retracted too. Fed a frozen training object cloud, the policy commanded ≤1.3 cm per step and never reached the bottle; the loop also ran 34× under rate. IK was not the problem.
  • →Object point quality is the new prime suspect. Bottle cloud z-extent > 30 cm on 14.2 % of fine-tune frames (median 8.3 cm). Port the --grad_thresh 0.010 flying-pixel filter onto the fine-tune extraction.
  • ·Tool coverage is thin. microwave 10 eps, spray 3 eps; sweep and pour_coffee not run.

07Caveats on the per-hand numbers

What the 29.2 / 0 / 0 does and does not support.

  • →0 % is a floor, not a point estimate. N = 24, one object, one seed, one num_envs. Inspire's 29.2 % is credible because it reproduces the documented baseline bit-for-bit; the zeros only say "never once succeeded in 24 tries", and their cause is what the diagnostics establish.
  • ·The new hands were given Inspire's finger gains. PD gains and torque limits were matched to Inspire deliberately — optimistic for 20/22 independent joints, and moot here since they never reach the object.
  • ·Object points were verified live, not cached. Recomputed every 100 steps through the harness's own call: 128/128 populated, camera-depth extent 8.43 cm at t=0 and never above 8.5 cm — no smear, no --override-obj-pts anywhere. This was the killer in the retracted attempt below.
  • ·No control-rate issue in this harness. parallel_eval is a synchronous batch loop — the sim advances only when the policy returns, so the 34× rate violation from the retracted run cannot occur. Throughput 31.4 / 59.9 / 57.9 env-steps/s.
  • ·Minor asymmetries between hands. The new hands' fingers start at 0 rad and their occluder meshes are lower-resolution; palm self-occlusion is absent for all three equally.

08Next

  • ·Scale-canonicalise the 6 points on both sides. Section 04 shows retargeting alone is not enough — the observation is six points too, so a small hand is OOD on input. Normalising fingertip offsets by hand reach on both the action and the observation, then re-running this comparison, is the direct test of D1 Option A.
  • ·Port the depth-gradient filter to the fine-tune data. Re-extract object points with --grad_thresh 0.010 and re-measure the smear rate; then check whether eval success moves. Running — 2048 pts/object with sensor-metric depth fusion; the paired check on the first episodes already shows the >30 cm smear rate going 22.9 % → 0.0 %.
  • ·Finish the Tool sweep. Run tactile on sweep + pour_coffee for a complete PnP / BMW / Tool picture; more spray episodes.
  • ·Fix the Tool reference labels. The thumb-free manual GT understates agreement; either re-label or report Tool against proximity only.
  • ·Tune the Sharpa wrist mount. Its flange orientation reuses the Wuji quaternion; a small adjustment gives a natural grasp pose.
