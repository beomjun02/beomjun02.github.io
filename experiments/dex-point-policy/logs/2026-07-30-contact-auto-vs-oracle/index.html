Experiment log · Dexterous Point Policy

Rebuilding the object points triples sim success; which contact label is better depends on the object

Two changes were made to the fine-tune data at once and then separated: the object point field was rebuilt (128 → 2048 points, mask erosion → depth-gradient rejection, sensor-metric depth fusion), and the per-finger contact label was swapped from the curated human annotation to the learned tactile prediction. Two otherwise identical finetunes, both scored by the openarm-sim benchmark's own success_at_end.

Verdict

The object-point rebuild is a real win; the contact-label question does not have a clean answer. Averaged over the three deployment objects at 512 episodes each, the rebuilt 2048-point metric field scores 36.8 % (learned-tactile label) and 31.7 % (human label) against 23.2 % for the shipped 128-point shrink20 pipeline — the clouds were a real bottleneck, exactly as the smear and object-size measurements predicted. But the two labels trade places by object: the human label wins bottle 76.6 % to 49.0 % and loses box 14.6 % to 44.7 % and ball 3.9 % to 16.6 %. Since each checkpoint was selected on bottle alone, the bottle-only sweep — which showed the human label ahead 49.5 % to 14.5 % on average — was measuring its own selection criterion. No claim about which label is better survives the other two objects.

What does survive is a measurement that reframes the question: the human annotation is one grasp bit replicated across all five fingers (1.21 distinct finger columns out of 5 over 69 k frames), so the contact head fits it at 0.98 accuracy while the genuine 5-bit tactile target only reaches 0.70 — with identical action loss between the runs. And one methodological finding cuts across every number here and in the previous log: this benchmark has four initial conditions, not 512, because the driver runs with zero position jitter.

02What was rebuilt, and why it mattered more than expected

The old field was produced by eroding every SAM3 mask inward by 20 px and back-projecting stereo depth. The new one keeps the sensor as the metric authority, fills only its holes with scale-fitted VGGT-Omega depth, rejects pixels by depth gradient instead of erosion, and samples 2048 points on the mask grid.

For bottle, box, teddy and towel the human label has exactly one distinct column — a single grasp flag copied to all five fingers. Only the tennis ball has two (thumb/index/middle vs ring/pinky). So this ablation is not "automating an expert label"; it replaces a 1-bit label with a 5-bit one. Reviewer C's complaint that the 6-keypoint action reduces to a wrist plus five binary grippers is, for the manual labels, correct.

The winner flips. The human label takes bottle by 27.6 points and gives back 30.1 on box and 12.7 on ball. Means: 23.2 % baseline, 31.7 % human, 36.8 % learned tactile. And the shipped baseline is itself better than the human-label rebuild on box (43.0 % vs 14.6 %), so "the rebuild helps" is also object-dependent rather than uniform.

Every cell row is worth reading on its own: no pipeline solves cell 2 for bottle, the baseline solves cell 3 perfectly for box and never for ball, and auto's ball score is one cell carrying all of it (81/128 in cell 3, ~2/128 elsewhere). With four initial conditions these are four measurements, not 512.

Selection caveat, and it is the load-bearing one: each checkpoint was chosen as its variant's argmax on bottle, over the same four initial conditions used to score it. The bottle column therefore flatters both, and the box/ball columns are the only ones the selection did not touch. The bottle numbers did not regress between the 128-episode selection run and this 512-episode one (75.0 → 76.6 oracle, 49.2 → 49.0 auto) — because the extra episodes are more simulator-noise repeats of the same four poses, not new task instances.

06Why the automatic label loses — and two fixes that did not work

The training curves rule out the boring explanation first.

The trajectory head is equally well trained in both runs — action loss and per-horizon L1 match to three digits. The entire difference is the contact head, and it is exactly what section 03 predicts: a 1-bit target is nearly free to fit, a 5-bit one is not. At deploy the predicted bits do two jobs: they drive how far each finger curls (ContactBendGrip), and with use_contact_ar=true they are fed back as policy input. Two interventions follow, and neither rescued it:

  • →Fold the five predicted bits into one gripper close signal. DPP_CONTACT_REDUCE=or: auto s20 k 2.3 % → 16.4 %, but s40 k 8.6 % → 6.2 %. Helps at one step, not the other, and nowhere near oracle — with four conditions this is most likely noise, not a mechanism.
  • →Also fold the autoregressive contact feedback. DPP_CONTACT_REDUCE=orall: auto s20 k 3.9 % — worse than both. The exposure-bias story (policy conditioning on its own noisy contact prediction) is not supported.

So the correlation between contact-head accuracy and deployed success is solid, and the causal path from one to the other is not established. That is the open question this log leaves behind, and it is a better place to be than a plausible untested story.

07The benchmark has four initial conditions, not 512

Found while reading the per-cell numbers, and it changes how every success rate on this harness should be quoted — including the ones already published.

The eval driver constructs its initial-state table as InitialStateTable(manifest, device=env.device) (eval.py:104), and that class defaults to xy_jitter_m = 0.0 (init_table.py:60). The manipuland is therefore placed at the exact grid-cell centre in every episode. With four cells, a 512-episode run samples four distinct task instances, 128 times each.

The repeats are not identical — final object positions differ by 16–30 cm within a cell and 121–126 of 128 are distinct — but that spread is PhysX slot-to-slot divergence, i.e. simulator noise, not task variation. It is why several cells come out at exactly 32/32 or 0/32. Consequences:

  • →A binomial CI on n = 512 is wrong. It describes sensitivity to simulator noise at four fixed poses, not generalisation. Every interval in results/TABLES.md is now mean ± 1.96 SE over the four cells, which is wide — honestly so.
  • →Per-cell numbers are the primary read. The shipped baseline's 25.4 % is 68/128 · 30/128 · 0/128 · 32/128 — one grid position it never solves in 128 attempts, which the aggregate hides completely.
  • ·The fix is one argument. xy_jitter_m > 0 is already implemented and just never exposed on the CLI. Turning it on gives genuine task-level sampling and is the first thing to do before any of these numbers go in a paper.

08The flip, on screen

Separate render passes at 8 seeds per grid cell, scored the same way. Their own success_at_end tracks the 512-episode runs closely (oracle bottle 22/32 vs 76.6 %, box 4/32 vs 14.6 %, ball 1/32 vs 3.9 %; auto box 14/32 vs 44.7 %, ball 7/32 vs 16.6 %), so these are representative episodes, not selected highlights.

The clearest single pair in the study: identical scene, identical initial pose, two checkpoints that differ only in which contact label trained them, and a 128-vs-0 split in that cell. Whatever the mechanism turns out to be, it is not subtle.

09Caveats

  • →Neither finetune ran the baseline's schedule. The baseline is 410 k steps at 128 points; these are ~100–175 k at 2048 points, which is ~2.6× slower per step. Both variants are compared at identical steps, but neither is compared to the baseline at equal steps.
  • →Baseline vs rebuilt is a pipeline comparison, not a controlled one. The baseline is evaluated at 128 points because that is what it was trained on, and the new runs at 2048 for the same reason (density parity). Point count, cleaning method and depth source all changed together; this log does not separate them.
  • →Checkpoint selection is on the evaluation conditions. With four conditions there is no held-out split. Sweep means, not best points, are the defensible summary.
  • ·The contact pos-weight differs between variants. 3.687 (auto) vs 2.478 (oracle) — computed from the labels by the trainer, so it is part of the treatment rather than a free knob, but it is not held constant.
  • ·Density parity is measured, not assumed. Visible-candidate fraction averaged 0.326 of 8192 mesh candidates ≈ 2674 real points, above the 2048 requested; on the worst-occluded frames (0.054) padding does occur.

10Next

  • ·Turn on position jitter and re-run the headline. Four initial conditions is the weakest part of every number here; xy_jitter_m already exists.
  • ·Isolate what the data rebuild bought. 128 vs 2048 points at fixed cleaning, and erosion vs depth-gradient at fixed count — two finetunes that split the 25 % → 75 % jump into its parts.
  • ·Establish or kill the contact-head causal path. Train the auto variant with the contact head disabled, and separately with the tactile label OR-reduced to one bit at training time rather than at deploy. Either isolates the channel properly.
  • ·Finish the step schedule. Both variants to a matched 400 k so the comparison sits where the baseline does.
