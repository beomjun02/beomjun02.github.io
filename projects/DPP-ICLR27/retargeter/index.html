Distilling Functional Transfer into a Reusable Hand Retargeter

Working note · v1.2, 2026-08-09 RESULTS v1 · n=29 held-out

ABSTRACT Existing human→robot hand retargeting is either pose-based — cheap and reusable but blind to the object — or object-based (functional) — correct, but locked inside per-demo/per-task RL policies that are never reusable. We distill the latter into the former's form: a small per-hand retargeter R (human hand keypoints → robot joint commands, closed-loop, per-step) and its inverse R⁻¹ (robot joints → human-equivalent hand), trained purely supervised on time-aligned human↔robot pairs mined from ManipTrans' released functional transfers (DexManipNet: 3.3K episodes, 1.2K objects, 1.34M frames), with no robot demonstrations and no task data. The load-bearing experiment holds architecture fixed and varies only the pairing criterion: the same R trained on kinematic pairs versus functional pairs. First results (n=29 held-out sequences): functional pairing wins on grasp robustness — retention 0.90–0.92 vs 0.78–0.79, object-drop at frame ~70 vs ~30, replay success 31–34% vs 28% @5 cm — while median tracking is similar; the effect is concentrated in the failure tail, not the typical sequence.

1  Motivation

Pose-based retargeting ignores the object and demonstrably fails across morphologies; the functional alternatives are correct but never yield a reusable module (one policy per demo or per task). We amortize the functional objective into the reusable form: distilled once, frozen across tasks — no robot demonstrations, no task data. Evidence and the full taxonomy: Related Works.

2  Method

R  :  ( k̂_k, q_k, u_{k−1} )  →  ( u_k = g_E(k̂_k) + Δq_k ,  Δwrist_k )
R⁻¹:  ( q_k )                →  ( k̃_k ,  Δ̃wrist_k )

Human hand = 21 keypoints; robot hand = independent joints q. Three commitments: (i) residual over off-the-shelf kinematic retargeting g_E; (ii) proprioceptive conditioning (q, uk−1) — what makes R a policy, not a converter; (iii) wrist as a learned correspondence, not position-pinning. The object never enters the observation — only the objective. I/O spec: Appendix A.

Pairs: DexManipNet episodes are time-aligned functional pairs by construction (residual tracking follows the source clock); the kinematic comparison set is g_E applied to the same source streams — only the pairing criterion differs. Hand-identity audit and ingestion gates: Appendix B.

Training: pure behavior cloning (L1 + fingertip-FK + regularizers); no RL, no DAgger. Losses and grids: Appendix C.

3  Experimental Setup

The central comparison is R-main vs R-kin ⭐ — identical architecture, functional vs kinematic pairs. Supporting arms (floor g_E, open-loop R-chunk, R-direct, R-W16, oracle ceiling): Appendix D. Evaluation is two-level — pair-space error (Level 1) and functional replay in ManipTrans' own simulator with a perturbation probe (Level 2); protocol: Appendix E.

Hypotheses — H1: functional pairing beats kinematic pairing (R-main > R-kin; the claim). H2: learning beats optimization alone (> g_E). H3: per-step feedback matters (> R-chunk, esp. acquisition/perturbation). H4: distillation loss is small (≈ oracle ceiling).

4  Results

Run of 2026-08-08→09. Pairs: 1,258 rollout sequences (492 OakInk-V2 source sequences; FAVOR excluded — no released human stream), 12-DOF command labels, split by source hash 90/10. All seven ingestion gates passed (frame count matches the paper's 1.34M exactly; temporal join certified to 1.1 mm; Appendix B). Two gate findings sharpened the method: commanded targets carry press-intent DC offsets under contact (validating command-space labels + L1), and the RL rollouts do not respect mimic coupling (so labels stay in the 12-DOF simulator convention; the 6-DOF projection moves to deploy).

Findings. H1 (functional > kinematic pairing): supported, with a precise shape — on the median sequence all retargeters track similarly (12.6–15.0 cm; the demo plan does most of the work), but kinematic arms fail catastrophically in the tail: R-kin drops objects at frame ~32 (g_E: ~27) versus ~67–79 for functional arms, and its mean error is 2.8× R-direct's. Functional pairing buys grasp robustness, not median precision. H2 (> g_E): yes on every column, same tail-shaped effect. H3 (per-step feedback): inconclusive — R-chunk's seg-end median matches R-direct, its mean is tail-inflated, and the perturbation probe (init-q noise, open-loop) moved no median metric at n=29 (a null result as measured). H4 (≈ oracle): not yet — 34% vs 79% @5 cm; the distillation gap is the main open problem, and W16's Level-2 win (despite a neutral Level-1) says temporal context in the input is one lever. Full open-loop replay depresses even the oracle to 10% @5 cm (med 19.7 cm) — quantifying why plan-level feedback (the frozen πH re-planning from fresh observations) is where closed-loop object correction must come from in the full system; auxiliary tables: Appendix E.

5  Discussion & Limitations

  • n=29, one split — success-rate differences are inside the binomial CI; the tail-shaped H1 evidence (retention, drop-time, mean error) is the defensible claim. Scale-up to the full val set and an unseen-object split is the immediate next run.
  • Distillation gap — best R is at 34% vs oracle 79% @5 cm. W16's win says input temporal context matters; the gap, not H1, is now the research problem.
  • Fixed-plan evaluation — replaying recorded human streams is open-loop at plan level by construction; closed-loop object correction belongs to the frozen πH (fresh observations each chunk) and is untested here.
  • Human side = OakInk-V2 raw_mano (matches πH's MANO provenance), not ManipTrans' SMPLX-derived reference — a deliberate provenance choice, unmeasured Δ.
  • Single hand so far — Wuji/Sharpa pending; Inspire-first is the conservative test (kinematic retargeting is strongest there). R⁻¹ not yet trained.
  • Hardware coupling unverified until rig experiments; labels live in the simulator's 12-DOF convention, the 6-DOF physical projection is a deploy-time step (Appendix B).
  • Composition with the frozen human policy is out of scope — see the base-policy workstream.
  • GPL-3.0 upstream (research use fine; release plans must account for it).

Appendix A  Interfaces & I/O specification

R   (per control tick):
  in  : k̂_k ∈ ℝ⁶⁰ (wrist-relative 20 keypoints, from the policy/source stream)
        q_k ∈ ℝ⁶  (measured independent joints)      u_{k−1} ∈ ℝ⁶ (previous command)
  out : u_k = g_E(k̂_k) + Δq_k ∈ ℝ⁶                    Δwrist_k ∈ ℝ⁶ (tanh-bounded)
  wrist path: robot wrist target = T_static(ŵ_k) ⊕ Δwrist_k → downstream IK (outside R)

R⁻¹ (per obs tick):
  in  : q_k ∈ ℝ⁶
  out : k̃_k ∈ ℝ⁶⁰ via internal θ̃ ∈ ℝ⁴⁵ → FK(θ̃, β_canon)   (anatomically valid by construction)
        Δ̃wrist_k ∈ ℝ⁶ ;  human wrist = T_static⁻¹(robot wrist) ⊖ Δ̃wrist_k
  post: EMA filter + joint-limit clamp
  • Why keypoints (not MANO θ) as the interface: the pretrained point-track policy trunk, dex-retargeting's native input, ManipTrans' 21-keypoint correspondence, and EgoDex's ARKit joints all speak points; θ appears only inside R⁻¹'s decoder head for validity.
  • Why q AND uk−1: u is what was commanded, q is what happened; their gap is a contact/lag signal (e.g., a finger stalled on the object surface). uk−1 additionally provides continuity and disambiguates label multimodality.
  • Semantic-channel symmetry: hand 60↔6 and wrist 6↔6 mirror exactly; the conditioning inputs are deliberately asymmetric (R is a policy; R⁻¹ is an encoder whose contract is consistency over accuracy — hence q-only input and output smoothing).
  • W variants: R-main uses W=1 (the current-step target only — the tracker-class standard: proprio, not lookahead, separates trackers from converters); R-W16 feeds the full committed chunk as input while keeping per-step output; R-chunk emits the whole chunk open-loop (plan-level and execution-level feedback are distinct layers — a fixed plan does not imply open-loop motor control).
  • T_static: per-hand reach-scale calibration of the wrist correspondence, initialized from our fingertip-vector alignment fit (validated on Inspire: reproduces dex-retargeting to 7.68° vs 7.7°). The human-side wrist is a canonical representation; loop consistency (R∘R⁻¹ ≈ id on the wrist channel) is the requirement, not physical coincidence — which is what accommodates differing hand scales (measured fingertip reach: Inspire 16.6 / Sharpa 12.2 / Wuji 8.5 cm).

Resolution: robot labels are defined in the 6 independent joints, whose geometry is identical across all conventions; each simulator/hardware expands 6→12 with its own coupling. The released dataset uses the inspire hand class (Inspire FTP's URDF is not redistributable and ships no public checkpoints).

Ingestion gates (all pass/fail before training): ① per-episode hand tag = inspire; ② action semantics — commands stored vs joint states only (else qt+1 proxy); ③ floating-base root pose present (Δwrist* labels); ④ object-pose trajectories present (functional verification); ⑤ join-alignment spot check on sampled episodes (frame rate/clock vs source datasets); ⑥ joint-limit-exceed fraction under our limits; ⑦ fingertip-FK discrepancy across coupling conventions over the dataset's q-distribution (mm).

Appendix E  Evaluation protocol (as run) & budget

  • Splits (as run): single pre-registered split by source-sequence hash 90/10 (seed 0) — unseen recordings, not unseen tasks/objects; the two-axis split is future work. Selection on validation only.
  • Level 1: joint-command L1 (rad), fingertip FK (mm), Δwrist L1. R⁻¹ was not trained in v1.
  • Level 2 (as run): ManipTrans' own IsaacGym env, rebuilt from their vis class with the training config's contacts (contact_offset 0.005, frictions 4.0/2.0, 8 pos-iters — the vis defaults eject grasped objects). Wrist = kinematic root tracking ("perfect arm"), fingers = PD position targets at 60 Hz. Stored rollouts are closed-loop RL output, so any fixed-plan replay diverges chaotically in contact; the primary protocol is therefore segmented replay (object+hand reset to recorded state every 60 frames; per-segment scoring), with full open-loop replay reported as an auxiliary divergence/retention measure. Held/drop use a 30 cm object-to-hand band.
  • Perturbation probe (as run): uniform ±0.3 rad initial-q noise under full open-loop replay. Result: no median metric moved at n=29 — a null as measured; the planned mid-rollout object wrenches remain future work.
  • Budget (actual): 19 training runs ≈ 1.6 h + replay sweep (13 arms × 29 seqs, one IsaacGym process per sequence) ≈ 3 h; total well under 1 A100-day. IsaacGym Preview 4 on A100 only; single-GPU jobs throughout.
Auxiliary — full open-loop replay (no resets), medians over n=29; means are dominated by flung-object outliers and are not reported here.
